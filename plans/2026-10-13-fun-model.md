## What the data says (OSM extract of 2026-10-13, measured, not guessed)

**(a) Posted limits.** 24,000 km of paved road outside motorways carries `maxspeed ≤ 50`. Only ~220 km say "urban" in
any tag. About 4,700 km of it is curvy (≥ 200°/km over ≥ 300 m); 4,170 km of those are unlit, without a sidewalk and
more than 600 m from any town, village or city centre: mountain roads where the limit warns about the bends (Mare de
Déu del Mont, C-462, GIV-5221, AS-352, LU-P-1901, JA-7155…). Lit roads, roads with a sidewalk and roads within 600 m
of a place number ~580 km of curvy road and really are built-up.

**(c) `unclassified`.** 117,000 km of paved `unclassified`. 2,068 km have a `ref` (938 km curvy), 7,021 km are named
Carretera/Estrada/Errepidea/Carreterín (4,095 km curvy). Together 9,100 km, 8 % of the class, and ~5,000 km of curvy
road: far more than the "400–500 roads" of the ROADMAP. 81,000 km are unnamed and 17,000 km are named Camino, Camí,
Pista…: those stay at a quarter.

**(b) Residential.** The pipeline drops `highway=residential` (it is not in `HIGHWAY_TO_CLASS`). The extract has 1.16 M
residential ways, 154,000 km: taking them all would add about half again to the road network (the other classes
are 320 MB of pbf against 90 MB of residential), so the graph cannot take them whole. What matters is a handful: the
extract has 3,095 dead ends of named or numbered roads (a way of at least 1.5 km). Of those, a single residential way
of at most 600 m joins a dead end to another road node in only 296 cases (65 km); 219 (33 km) within 300 m and 106
(9 km) within 150 m. Vidrà (Carrer de Ciuret, w737018081, 100 m) is one of them. Plain "touches a dead end" would keep
10,448 ways (2,593 km), mostly urban cul-de-sacs, so the road's own length and name are part of the rule.

## The plan

### Step 1 — (a) a low limit is urban only where the road is built-up

`fun_weight` keeps its urban rule, but the rule now needs evidence that the road is in a built-up area. A way with
`maxspeed ≤ 50` has no fun when at least one holds:

1. an explicit urban tag (`maxspeed=ES:urban`, or `source:maxspeed`, `zone:maxspeed`, `maxspeed:type` saying urban);
2. `lit=yes`;
3. a `sidewalk` tag (any value but `no`/`none`);
4. its middle lies within `BUILT_UP_RADIUS_M` (600 m) of a city, town or village node (the pipeline already reads
   them for the place search).

Else the limit is ignored for fun, as the comment at `roads.py:73` says. The travel time still honours the limit
(the planner must not think a 30 km/h road is a 90 one). Pure function `is_built_up(way, places)`, a flag on `Way`,
no new data and no size change.

Not a magic number: 600 m is the radius that leaves 317 km of curvy road urban near villages while the 700 km of curvy
road 600–1,500 m from a village centre stays mountain; it is tuned on the bench, not argued.

### Step 2 — (c) `unclassified` with a ref or a road name is tertiary

At parse time, `unclassified` with a `ref`, or a name starting with Carretera, Ctra., Estrada, Errepidea, Carreterín,
becomes `TERTIARY`. Cruising speed 45 → 60 km/h and pace factor ¼ → 1: the same edges, the same size. The name list is
a named constant. Camino, Camí, Pista, Senda, Vereda, Cañada stay at a quarter, and tracks stay closed (the
`is_paved` heuristics run first). Risk: a farm lane named "Carretera": R10 (suspicious names) must not grow, and the
bench shows the roads gained and lost by name (D3).

### Step 3 — (b) residential connectors

`highway=residential` enters the pre-filter, which is cached by the filter, so the first run re-filters the extract (about a minute for residential).
`select_connectors(ways)` keeps a residential way when, at once:

1. it is a single way of at most `CONNECTOR_MAX_M` (600 m), not an area, with no access restriction and paved;
2. one end is an end of a non-residential way that has a `ref` or a name and is at least `CONNECTOR_MIN_ROAD_M`
   (1,500 m) long, and that end touches no other way (a dead end);
3. the other end is a node of another way.

Kept connectors become class `RESIDENTIAL` (new value 8, 30 km/h, fun 0, never a twisty), so the graph and `tracks.bin`
stay at the same size give or take ~300 edges (+0.03 %). The Python `RoadClass` and the app's `RoadClass` get the value;
the app counts it as "other road" (`ride.ts`), and may snap a point to it like to any road. Everything else about residential stays out. Chains of several residential ways are
left out until the bench shows a through road they would fix: the plan reports how many of the 3,095 dead ends remain
after the rule so that decision is data.

## What each step is judged on

Each step is its own branch off `main`, the pipeline run into its own output directory from the same pinned
extract (`data/raw/spain-latest.osm.pbf`, 2026-10-13), the bench pointed at it with `TWISTY_DATA`. A different data
hash: the diff judges twisty km and roads, not the fun yardstick.

1. **G1: no great road found before is lost, none lost per trip.** Recall of 29 of 118 must rise. Expected movers:
   (a) C-462 ×2, GIV-5221, BV-5114, CA-643, Mare de Déu del Mont (dead end), N-625 León half; (c) JV-7032, Tost,
   Santa Engràcia; (b) Vidrà.
2. **The six named roads, before and after** (graph fun, overlay, demanded, found): Mare de Déu del Mont, GIV-5221,
   C-462, BV-5114, Vidrà, JV-7032.
3. **D3 roads gained and lost by name**, read by hand for the top 12 on each side; **D2** twisty km per budget; **D4**.
4. **Sanity checks must not grow**: R10 suspicious names (40 trips today), R6 and R6b (urban crawl and towns crossed:
   step 1 must not send trips through villages), R1/R2, R4, R12, R14.
5. **G6** (rated roads within 30 min that no stop rides): 121 of 127 trips today; it should fall, not rise.
6. **Cost**: settled nodes per plan (mean 1,621k, p95 5,872k), Mac ms, and **size**: `graph.bin` (42.3 MB),
   `tracks.bin` (125 MB), `twisties.json` (2.6 MB), node and edge counts, the APK. Memory on the Huawei if the graph
   grows more than 5 %: Madrid → Granada time (3.0 s today) and the worker's heap on the device.

A step that loses a great road, or raises R10 or the graph size without paying in found roads, is changed or dropped,
not kept.

## Order and cost

Steps 1 and 2 first (no size change, ~half a day each with the pipeline run of ~15 min in a heavy slot), step 3
last (the only one that can grow the graph). `make data` from the pinned extract runs once per step. Each step ends
with the new `baseline.jsonl`, the diff summary in the commit body, and the sizes. The tester APK is the director's.
